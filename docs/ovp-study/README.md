# Decision: monitor VSEL independently and disable all five outputs

**Future isolated-DC revision only. Proceed to a dummy-load prototype, not to candle use.** A separate voltage detector can recognize a sustained high regulator output without trusting U1's ADJ node. Use the existing branch switches as disconnects. This costs no extra series voltage drop. It does **not** establish that a candle survives the voltage reached before turn-off, or that the selected voltage is the one the candle needs. If either guarantee is required, do not release this design on the evidence here.

Baseline: [E1 operating contract, regulator budget and net intent](../../hardware/e1/README.md), revision `d00a20b`. Five common-return outputs, 20 mA nominal / 50 mA continuous per branch. U1 is ST **LD1117V** (TO-220: 1 ADJ, 2 OUT and tab, 3 IN; [ST datasheet, Fig. 2 and Table 8](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/ld1117.pdf)). Keep its feedback, minimum load and 22 µF tantalum at OUT. Do not insert a disconnect in the feedback path. A fixed high-mode ceiling misses a regulator stuck at 4.5 V in low mode; shutting down only U1 cannot interrupt an IN–OUT short inside U1.

The monitor and its reference are independent of the regulator loop, **not** of the board ground, branch switch silicon, mode request or source. Separate high-permission shunt on the test fixture; alternatively use electrically isolated poles of one selector. Do not bridge the original JP1 pins to the monitor contact. De-energize, discharge **all** outputs, set both contacts, and meter the unloaded outputs before attaching loads. No live mode changes.

In regulation the released EN bus is at least 2.895 V to rounding (2.923 V minus 10.1 kΩ × (5 × 0.5 + 0.3) µA); VIH is 1.1 V. At 6.15 V fault input the 9.9 kΩ pull-up sources at most 0.622 mA, plus at most 2.5 µA from EN pins; compare OUTB's 0.25 V maximum at 5 V / 5 mA with VIL = 0.66 V.[1][2] Check logic levels at every switch. Do not use the 210 kΩ limit resistors, FAULT flags or regulator current limiting as voltage detectors.

## DC windows at the monitored node

With upper resistance `T` and lower conductance `G`, Kirchhoff's current law gives `VSEL_trip = Vsense × (1 + T × G) + Iin × T`, positive current into the IC. `G = 1/10k` low; add `1/(18.2k + Rcontact)` high. TI specifies rise 396–404 mV and fall 387–400 mV over temperature. The 5.5 mV typical hysteresis is **not** a guaranteed noise allowance.[1] Model each resistor independently at ±0.20% (0.1% initial + 25 ppm/K × 40 K), high contact 0–1 Ω, and total signed midpoint leakage ±50 nA. Leakage is a design allowance to verify, not a manufacturer guarantee at threshold: TI lists ±15 nA for INB− at 0.1 V.[1] No sense filter fitted; adding one changes the fault delay. Wiring drops, ground shift, contamination and ripple must fit the measured margin.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|Low|2.923–3.077|3.212–3.308|3.139–3.276|134 mV|
|High|4.378–4.604|4.761–4.902|4.653–4.853|156 mV|

\*Outward rounding to mV. `python3 check.py` in this directory enumerates endpoint combinations, checks current balance and separation using guarded normal maxima of 3.078/4.605 V. Rise and fall intervals describe a population, not an inverted hysteresis on one device. Both modes can release at their normal voltage after a fault; neither latches. The low maximum rising threshold is below the high minimum normal rail. The high maximum rising threshold is below a hard pass-through of a healthy 6.05 V input. **Neither maximum is a maximum output voltage.** No allowed pulse amplitude, duration, energy or even continuous maximum has been established for an actual candle.

**Headroom and current.** Preserve E1's 6.05–6.15 V at J1 under load, including ripple, and ≤20 mV loss to U1 IN. At the guarded high maximum, `6.05 − 0.020 − 4.605 = 1.425 V`, only 25 mV above ST's 1.4 V reference-test differential. An added upstream series switch could spend that margin; the proposed enable control does not. Branch loss remains up to 6.75 mV at 50 mA before connector/harness losses.[2] The detector's 1.8–18 V operating range covers VIN; a bounded 6.15 V pass-through remains within the switch's 2.5–6.5 V operating range, **not** permission for an unbounded source transient.[1][2] Allow 20 µA detector current (TI lists 13 µA max at 5 and 12 V), divider <87 µA and fault-state pull-up <622 µA: <0.73 mA extra, even counting mutually exclusive loads together.[1] E1's ~376 mA single-short budget thus remains below 0.40 A on paper. Recheck actual source foldback, U1 heating, regulator stability and healthy-branch dips.

## What a fault actually does

|Condition|Expected result / limit of coverage|
|---|---|
|Feedback fault, regulator pass-through or regulator selector stuck high, independent low permission intact|If VSEL crosses the applicable threshold long enough with VIN valid, OUTB disables all functioning switches. Faulted VSEL and the input-side caps **remain live**. A sustained excess below the threshold is not detected.|
|Regulator high, monitor low (including open high-permission contact)|All branches disabled once detected; nuisance loss of service and possible initial pulse. Regulator low, monitor high instead: normal 3 V runs, **latent loss of low-mode protection**. This mismatch is not diagnosed.|
|One common selector jams in high, a shared high command is wrong, or operator selects high for a 3 V load|Both paths can validly request high. No voltage-only test distinguishes that state from intended high operation. Two electrical poles are not independent evidence of load rating. Even separate shunts do not identify a load. **Not covered.**|
|71.5 kΩ or sense trace open; midpoint shorted to return; OUTB open; EN short to VSEL; branch pass FET short|Can leave one or all branches energized; a shorted EN bus can also overload OUTB. Not a single-fault-tolerant safety disconnect. A downstream external supply/backfeed is outside scope.|
|Power-up, monitor supply lost, brownout or intermittent regulator|OUTB is high impedance in UVLO; outputs can be indeterminate below POR, and TI allows 450 µs after VDD exceeds 1.8 V for correct output.[1] EN is pulled **on**, not fail-off, if monitor output floats. No guarantee against an initial pulse or repeated re-enables. If manual reset or start-inhibit is required, reject this circuit as drawn.|

## Stored charge and response time

C2 and the five switch input bypasses (nominal sum 27 µF) remain **upstream**. They can supply fault current during detection and switch turn-off; the lab supply's 0.40 A setting does not bound that pulse. Cable inductance and source output capacitance matter. TPS3700 propagation figures in §6.6 apply at 10 mV sense overdrive and specified loading; very short pulses can be rejected.[1] TPS2553 specifies turn-off up to 3 ms with **1 µF and 100 Ω**, not an arbitrary load. Its ~2 µs typical short-current response is not an EN turn-off time.[2] Do not add typical delays to claim a guaranteed peak. A fast step can reach nearly the fault rail on an enabled lightly loaded branch before EN goes low.

Downstream, E1 fits no intentional bulk capacitor, but the cable and candle can add one. As a scale example, net 100 mA into 100 µF adds 1 V in 1 ms (`ΔV = ∫I dt / C`); that capacitor holds 1.89 mJ at 6.15 V (`CV²/2`). Neither number is a fault bound. Small C can rise faster; large C can hold the excess after opening and cause inrush on restart. TPS2553DBVR does not remove stored charge.[2] With no defined discharge load there is no guaranteed time to a safe voltage. On a resistive fixture use `t = RC ln(Vstart/Vend)` only if no other feed remains. Measure at the far end of each lead, not just at EN or VSEL.

## Bench verification gate — proposed, not performed

1. Use a **separate low-voltage fixture**, no changes to E1, isolated current-limited DC, no mains, batteries or candles. Confirm exact suffixes/pin numbers, selector truth table and continuity to each EN unpowered. Start at ≤50 mA, open outputs. For fault emulation, physically isolate the regulator OUT from the fixture rail; power the detector from a valid 6.10 V channel and drive VSEL from a second bounded isolated DC channel with common return. Never drive into U1 or parallel supply outputs. Check both channels' current limits and overshoot into dummy loads first. Cap the fault stimulus at 6.15 V at the fixture, stop if exceeded.
2. With monitor supply already valid, ramp VSEL slowly through each window up and down; measure VSEL with calibrated DMM, scope VSEL, INB− (high impedance), EN and branch OUT. Record crossings, instrument uncertainty and contact state. Check all five EN levels/outputs. Each transition must agree with its DC interval within documented uncertainty; an ambiguous result needs a better measurement. Hold above trip: no sustained sourcing into a resistive load. Return below release: document automatic restart. Exercise all four regulator/permission combinations, especially high/high with a dummy 3 V load designation: **expect no trip**.
3. On a future integrated fixture, verify actual normal rails and U1 differential at source extremes, unloaded, one then five × 20/50 mA loads (E1 resistor values or electronic loads). At full load use 0.40 A source limit. Scope start/stop, slow/fast VIN ramps, repeated brownouts and 0↔250 mA steps. Check for nuisance trips, oscillation and restarts. Briefly current-limit one branch with the other four loaded; measure fault current, healthy outputs and switch/regulator temperatures; stop on rapid heating or foldback.
4. On the isolated fault fixture repeat slow ramps and bounded fast steps from each setpoint toward 6.15 V, including power-up into an existing fault and pulses of varied duration. Characterize stimulus rise time/current at VSEL; a slow supply dial is not a fast-fault test. Repeat no load, rated resistive loads, shortest/longest intended leads, then known downstream capacitors (e.g. 1, 10, 100 µF; check polarity/rating). Capture VIN, VSEL, EN and each connector peak/duration with short probe returns and single-shot acquisition; repeat for far-end voltage and branch current. Record time from threshold to current interruption, voltage decay with and without a load, and repeated pulses on recovery. Remove power and meter residual voltage before every mode or wiring change.
5. Archive traces with bandwidth/sample rate, load, cable, capacitance, source limit, ramp, temperature and mode. Stop if normal operation reaches trip, a sustained fault still sources a load, or margins disappear with temperature/measurement error. Before **any** candle use, obtain its permitted DC and transient envelope, startup current, polarity and battery-impedance dependence; compare worst measured peak, dwell and repetition **with margin**. Unknown envelope, insufficient measured margin or a requirement to survive the common selector fault means **no approval**. A passed dummy-load test is not safety certification.
